# Using ElectroTag in the browser

This guide is for annotators. ElectroTag runs in your web browser: you open an EEG recording, mark
the artifacts you see with their type, and press Save and Finish. The recording stays on your own
computer and is read by the web page itself, so the EEG is never uploaded; only your annotations,
and a record of how you used the tools if your study asks for one, go to the study.

The application links to this guide from the sign-in page, under the greeting of your home page and
at the end of the Help tab of the annotation view; each link opens the guide in a new tab.

## Opening ElectroTag

Your study team sends you a link. Opening it shows the sign-in page of your study, with the study's
name under the heading. If the page says "Demonstration" instead, you are in the demonstration,
which needs no account and keeps everything in your browser (see the last section). If you were
given a study address rather than a link, choose "Taking part in a study? Enter its address" on the
sign-in page, type the address and choose Connect; the browser remembers it next time.

ElectroTag needs a screen the size of a tablet or larger: a computer with a mouse or trackpad and
a keyboard, or a tablet such as an iPad, held upright or on its side (the section "On an iPad"
below describes the touch controls). See "On a phone" below for what a phone shows.

## Signing in

Depending on how your study is run, you sign in with your annotator ID or with your email address,
and the password your study team gave you. Studies that use email addresses offer "Forgot
password?", which sends you a link to choose a new password. You stay signed in until you sign out
or close the browser tab. If your sign-in ends while you are working (after a long break, for
example), the page asks you to sign in again; your unsaved annotations are kept in the browser and
are offered to you when you open the recording again.

When a study records how its annotators use ElectroTag, a short notice says so after you sign in.
The record contains clicks, key presses and what is on the screen, never anything you type, and the
notice says so in those words: notes and passwords are never recorded.

Some studies send each annotator a private invitation link instead of a password. It opens a welcome
page with your annotator ID, where you choose your own password and start. The link works once, so
open it on the tablet or computer you will annotate on; on a phone the welcome page says first that
annotating needs a larger screen, and the email with the link says the same. A link that has been
used already leads to the sign-in. One that has expired, or was replaced by a newer one, says so and
asks you to contact the study team who invited you for a new link, for example by replying to the
email that brought it.

## Your recordings

The first page has one black button, Open next recording, for the recording to do next. When you
have a recording you started and did not finish, the card is headed In progress and the button
reads Continue where you left off instead, with how much of that recording you have viewed, your annotations so far, the time worked
and when the draft was last kept. Choose it and open the same file, and the recording opens as you
left it, without a question. Annotators in a training programme go straight to their training
path instead (see below).

Under it, Your progress shows how many of your recordings are done, with a thin bar, the time you
have worked in all and the median time per recording, and what you have done today. After your first
two recordings it also estimates the time left at your own pace so far: the time you took per minute
of recording, applied to the recordings still to do, less the time already spent on unfinished ones.
Recordings differ, so treat it as a guide. All these times leave out the time you were paused. The
time worked today is counted in the browser you work in, so it covers that browser only.

Below that is the list of the recordings assigned to you. It is made for a study in which each
annotator has a couple of hundred recordings. Its tabs
show what is still to do (To do holds every recording you have not finished, started or not, and
In progress only those you have started), what is done, all of them, and your History, each with
the number of recordings it holds. While your study recordings wait for your competency assessment,
they are listed under a Locked tab of their own rather than as recordings to do. A search field finds recordings by their file name, and Sort
orders the list by the recordings you worked on most recently (unfinished work first), by file
name, or by length. The list shows 25 recordings at a time, with Previous and Next below it. The
tab and the order you chose are remembered in this browser.

## History and revisions

The History tab lists every recording you have saved, the most recent first, with when it was
saved, its version, the number of annotations in it and the time you worked on it (for example "5 min",
as the progress card counts it). When you spot a
problem in a recording you have already finished, choose Open to revise beside it (or in the Done
tab), then open the same file from your computer as you did the first time. Opening a finished
recording with Open a recording from your computer offers Open to revise as well. The recording opens with
the annotations you saved most recently, and a blue banner under its name says that you are
revising it: "Revising version 1. Submitting saves your changes as version 2; version 1 stays as it
was saved."

Change what needs changing, then choose Submit the revision on the banner. Your changes are saved
as a new version beside the earlier one, which is kept exactly as it was, and the time you spend
revising is measured as usual. A revision may fix one place only, so Submit the revision does not
first list the parts of the recording not yet viewed. Analyses of the study use your latest version. Leave without
changes on the banner closes the recording and keeps every saved version as it is. A revision you
close part-way (Close, then Close and revise later) is kept as a draft, and the history says that a
revision is under way; Continue the revision picks it up again. You can revise a recording more
than once: each revision builds on the latest version.

Your study may switch revisions off, for example when the study's design needs each recording to be
annotated once without second thoughts. Saved recordings are then final, and Open to revise does
not appear. Recordings of the training programme keep the programme's own rules.

## When a recording cannot be annotated

Sometimes a recording cannot be judged as it is: the file will not open, it is the wrong
recording, channels are flat, missing or disconnected, or it is too noisy or too short to annotate.
Saving such a recording as having no artifacts would mix "clean" with "could not be judged", so tell
the study team instead. Choose Report a problem with this recording… from the More button (the
three dots) beside the recording in your list, which works even for a file that will not open, or
from the More button in the header of the annotation view. Choose what is wrong, add a note of up
to 300 characters if it helps (for example "Fp1 and Fp2 are flat from 2:10 on"), and choose what
happens next. No reason is chosen for you, and Send the report waits until you have chosen one; in
the annotation view, where the file has opened, "The file will not open" is not offered.

Tell the study team and carry on sends the report and leaves everything as it was, so you can go
on annotating the recording. This recording cannot be annotated sets it aside: the recording
counts as finished for your progress, is listed with your done recordings as Cannot be annotated,
and is kept apart from the recordings you saved, so that the study's analyses leave it out. A
recording opened from the annotation view closes when you set it aside, and the draft of what you
had marked is kept. Only an unfinished study recording can be set aside; for a recording you have
saved, or one of the training programme, the report tells the study team and you carry on.

The study team may resolve the report, give the recording to someone else, or give it back to you,
for example once they have replaced the file. A recording given back returns to your To do list,
and a short line at the top of your list says so, with the study team's note. A report is kept
once even when you press its button again, or try again after an answer that was slow to arrive.

## Reviews of your work

A lead annotator may review a recording you have saved, marking each of your annotations Agree,
Caveat or Comment, sometimes with a note, and adding a comment where an artifact may have been
missed. The review never changes your saved annotations. Your list and your history then say
"Reviewed: 2 caveats, 1 comment" under the recording's name.

To read the feedback, choose See the feedback… from the recording's More button, or the "Reviewed"
line itself, and open the same file from your computer. Your latest saved version opens to read, with the feedback in the side
panel and a calm blue mark under each annotation it is about (a dashed blue outline marks a stretch
without an annotation). N and P, or a tap on an item, go from one item to the next. When the study
takes revisions, Open to revise starts a revision of the recording, and the feedback stays in the
side panel while you revise; it is for you to judge what to change. Some studies keep reviews from
the annotators (blind reviews), and then none of this appears.

## Corrections of your work

An expert of the study may correct a recording you have saved: change a mark's type, its start or
end or its channels, remove a mark or add one you missed, with a reason for each change, such as
Missed artifact, Wrong type or Start or end, and sometimes a short note. A correction is a new
version of your work, credited to the expert; the version you saved stays exactly as it was, and
your list does not change. Your list and your history then say "Corrected by Expert one: 3 changes"
under the recording's name.

To see the correction, choose that line and open the same file from your computer. The corrected
version opens to read, with your own marks drawn as dashed outlines where they differ from it, and
the side panel lists each change with the expert's reason and note, in the order of the recording,
in calm blue. N and P, or a click on a change, go from one change to the next, and ? lists the keys.
When the study takes revisions and the correction is of your latest version, Open to revise starts a
revision of your work; it is for you to judge what to change. Some studies keep corrections from the
annotators, and then none of this appears.

Sometimes two experts correct the same version and disagree in places. Until the study has settled
where they disagree, your history says "Corrected by Expert one and Expert two, who disagree in
places: the study is settling it", and shows neither expert's version, so that you never learn from a
change the study may not keep. Once an expert has decided each place, the line reads "Corrected by
Expert one and Expert two, settled by Expert three: 2 changes", and the corrected version you open is
the settled one: each change gives the experts' reason where they agreed, and the decision and its
reason where they did not. Below the line, Where the experts disagreed shows each of those places
without opening the recording: what you had marked, what each expert made of it, what was kept and
why. Your own files stay exactly as you saved them.

## Decisions on your disagreements

Some studies give each recording to two or more people, each annotating it on their own, as a
systematic review has two people screen each record. Where your annotations and a colleague's
disagree, an expert looks at each place and decides which marks to keep. Once that is finished,
your home page has a card near its top, Decisions on your disagreements, which says first in how
many places the expert decided as you had it, and then lists each recording concerned: open it to
see each place, what you marked, what the others marked (nobody is named), what was kept, and the
expert's reason. Each place is told from your side, from what was kept: your mark was kept; there
is no mark, as you had it; both marks (or yours among others') were kept; a colleague's mark was
kept instead of yours, or where you had none; your mark was not kept; or the expert drew their own
mark. A blue line means what you did stood. In the History of your recordings, a line under the
recording ("Adjudicated: 9 of 17 disagreements decided as you had them") opens the card at that
recording. When two experts corrected a version of yours differently and another expert settled
it, the card lists that recording too, with each place where the experts disagreed and what was
kept. If the expert takes a second look, the decisions shown stay until they finish again.
Your own files stay exactly as you saved them; the decisions make a new version beside them. Some
studies keep these decisions from the annotators, and then the card does not appear.

## Progress and milestones

Many studies also show Goals and milestones beside your progress (your study can switch them off).
It shows the whole team's progress through the recordings it was given (except while you share the
study with just one other person, whose figures it would otherwise give away) and the number of
annotations you have made, for interest only. You can set yourself a goal for the week (a number of
recordings, counted from Monday), and a bar shows how far you are. Each milestone's badge says what
it stands for when you point at it or use a screen reader: a number for recordings saved, a number
over a small calendar for days in a row, a filled disc for recordings viewed in full, and a pie for
the team's share of the study; reached badges have a solid blue ring, the others a dashed grey one.

Milestones mark your progress through the study: your first recording, then 10, 25, 50, 100, 150,
200 and 300 recordings; saving recordings on 3, 5 and 10 days in a row; recordings viewed from start
to end (99% or more of a recording on the screen, as the Recording tab measures it); and the team reaching a quarter, half,
three quarters and all of its recordings. The line under your badges names the next recording
milestone only when the recordings assigned to you can reach it. The team's figure counts the
recordings saved; your own progress also counts those set aside because they cannot be annotated. When Save and Finish reaches a milestone, the message
after saving says so, and a short note with the milestone's badge appears above your milestones; it never stands between you and the next recording. Milestones reward finishing
recordings and looking through them in full. They never reward speed or the number of annotations:
mark what you see, as many or as few artifacts as there are. The number of annotations is shown for
interest only, and a revision does not count as a new recording.

The leaderboard ranks the people in your study by the recordings they have completed (or, in some
studies, by the recordings they viewed in full) and shows the top ten and your own place. Everyone
appears under a pseudonym made for the study, an adjective and an animal such as "Steady Heron", so
nobody sees anyone's annotator ID or name; yours is named above the table and highlighted in it. It
appears once at least three people are on it. Clear Show me on the leaderboard to leave it; you can
come back at any time.

## Opening a recording

ElectroTag asks you to choose the recording on your computer. Choose the file (an EDF or BDF
file), drop it onto the page, or choose the folder that holds it. For a BrainVision recording
choose its three files together (the .vhdr, .vmrk and .eeg), or their folder.

The page then reads the file and works out its fingerprint, a checksum of its contents, which takes
a few seconds for a large file and shows its progress. The fingerprint is what is sent to the study
to recognise the recording; the EEG itself is not. The study's answer appears in plain words. When
the file is the study's copy of one of your recordings, the recording opens. The steps and the
answer appear just under the place where you chose the file, and the page scrolls to the answer and
gives it the focus, so you see at once what happened. When the file has the
right name, sampling rate, length and channels but its contents differ from the study's copy (it
may have been converted or re-saved), ElectroTag explains the difference and lets you choose
between opening it anyway and choosing the study's copy; ask your study team if you are not sure.
A file that is not part of the study can still be looked at in try-out mode, which is clearly
labelled and saves nothing. A recording of the study that is not yours at the moment, or one you
have already finished, is not opened, and the page says which of the two it is.

When the page was opened for one recording (Open next recording, or a stage of your training path)
and you choose another of your recordings, it opens directly if both are ordinary study recordings.
If one of them belongs to the training programme, ElectroTag asks first, naming both, because a
study recording counts as real work: choose Open it anyway, or Choose another file. On a training
page the answers are worded for training, name the file that step needs, and Back returns to your
training path.

If the connection is lost before the page has loaded what it needs to read recordings, the page says
so and offers Try again; nothing is wrong with your file, and a copy kept on this device stays
there. A copy is removed only when its files can no longer be read as a recording. ElectroTag loads
these parts of the page as soon as you sign in to a study, so this rarely happens. When the study
cannot be reached to check a file, Try again asks it once more with the file already read.

The page fetches each of its parts (your recordings, the annotation view, training, the quiz, the
admin area) the first time you need it, and says "Loading…" while one is on its way on a slow
connection. If a part cannot be fetched, the page says so and offers Try again; your work is safe,
and reloading the page also helps if Try again does not.

Recordings of any size the study registered can be opened: ElectroTag has been checked with
recordings of 1 to 257 channels at up to 16384 samples a second. Working out the fingerprint of a
large file takes longer, about half a minute for a 2 GB recording of 256 channels, and the progress
is shown meanwhile. A BrainVision recording whose header gives the same name to two channels cannot
be opened; the page says which names, and the study team can make a corrected copy of the header.

If you worked on the recording before without finishing it, ElectroTag offers to restore your
unfinished work, with the number of annotations and the time spent. Choose Restore and continue, or
Start afresh to begin again. A restored draft opens exactly where you left off: the same moment of
the recording, the same channels, amount of time and trace size, and the parts you had already
viewed. A short line at the top of the Annotations tab welcomes you back
with where you were, the annotations kept and the time worked so far.

## Opening a recording from a link

Your study team may keep the recordings in Dropbox or OneDrive and send you a link to each one
instead of the file. Paste the link into Open from a link, under the place where you drop files,
and press Open the link. For a BrainVision recording paste the links of its three files, one per
line. The page fetches the file straight from Dropbox or OneDrive into this browser, showing how
much of it has arrived, and then reads it, checks its fingerprint and opens it exactly as if you
had chosen it on your computer. The file never passes through the study, and the study never sees
the link: the link is not stored, not sent anywhere but to the service that holds the file, and
not written to the interaction log.

The channel map can place the electrodes where the recording's own BIDS electrodes.tsv puts them.
With Dropbox or OneDrive, paste the links of the electrodes.tsv and its coordsystem.json on lines
of their own, after the recording's. For a recording at a plain web address, ElectroTag looks for
them itself, beside the recording at the same address, under their BIDS names (for
sub-01_ses-1_task-rest_eeg.edf, sub-01_ses-1_electrodes.tsv and then sub-01_electrodes.tsv, each
with its coordsystem.json). The recording opens straight away while they are fetched; if they are
not there, cannot be read or take more than a few seconds, the map uses the standard positions and
nothing else changes.

To share a file from Dropbox, choose Share on the file itself (not on its folder), make sure the
link is for anyone who has it, and choose Copy link. In OneDrive, choose Share, then Anyone with the
link can view, and copy the link. Links to other web addresses work too when the server that holds
the file allows web pages to read it. Treat a share link as you would the file: anyone who has it
can download the recording until its owner switches the link off, so send it only to the person
who annotates the recording and switch it off when the study no longer needs it.

Some links cannot be read by a web page. Google Drive never allows it, OneDrive for work or
school (SharePoint) usually does not, and a link that is not shared with everyone, has expired or
points to a folder fails too. ElectroTag then says what went wrong in plain words and offers
the next step in the same place: Download the file opens the link in a new tab, where your browser
downloads the file, and then Choose the downloaded file opens the usual file chooser so that you
can pick it from your Downloads folder (on an iPad, Downloads in the Files app). ElectroTag
cannot reach your Downloads folder, so delete the downloaded file there yourself when you have
finished with it: in the Files app on an iPad, press and hold the file and choose Delete; on a
Mac, drag it from Downloads in the Finder to the Bin; in Windows, select it in File Explorer and
press Delete.

## Recordings kept on this device

A recording opened from a link is kept on the device, in this browser's own storage for
ElectroTag, so that it opens again without downloading: the next time you open that recording,
ElectroTag finds the copy, checks its fingerprint as it does for any file, and opens it straight
away. Untick Keep a copy on this device under the link field if you would rather download it each
time. Files you choose on your computer are already on it and are not copied, unless you tick Also
keep files I choose here on this device (useful on an iPad, where the Files app may not keep a
downloaded file to hand). A recording's electrodes.tsv and coordsystem.json, when they came with it,
are kept with its copy, so that it opens again with the same positions on the channel map. Each copy
is filed under your study and your sign-in, so people who share a computer never see each other's
copies, and only the files themselves are kept: never the link they came from, because a link works
as a password for the file. In a private window, and in browsers
that keep nothing for a site, the tick boxes are replaced by a sentence saying that this browser
cannot keep recordings, and recordings open from the link or the computer each time.

The home page says how many recordings are kept and how much space they take, with Manage, and the
page that opens recordings has a button, Recordings on this device…; both show the kept recordings with their
sizes and whether you have finished them, the space this site uses and how much more the browser
allows, and the ways to free space: Remove next to each recording, Remove finished recordings, which
says how much it frees before it does so, and Remove all. A copy that a recording open in this or
another tab or window was opened from is kept, and the panel says so: the browser reads a recording's
files bit by bit as you view it, so close it there first. Tick Remove a recording from this device
when I finish it to have each copy removed once Save and Finish has saved your work. Removing a copy
never touches your annotations, drafts or results, which the study keeps. When the device is full,
the recording still opens, from memory, and ElectroTag offers to remove the recordings you have
finished. When the first recording is kept, ElectroTag asks the browser to keep the site's storage
even when space runs low (some browsers ask you, others decide for themselves); without that, a
browser short of space may clear the copies, and they are then downloaded again from the link.

When you sign out with recordings kept, ElectroTag asks whether to keep them or remove them.
Keeping them is the default, for your own device; on a computer that others use, remove them. The
copies also go when you clear this site's data in the browser's settings. Files you downloaded
yourself, with Download the file or in any other way, are in your Downloads folder, which
ElectroTag cannot reach: delete those in the Files app, the Finder or File Explorer as described
above.

## The annotation view

The EEG fills most of the window, one channel per row with its name on the left, the time along the
bottom and a scale bar for each type of channel on the right. Above the traces is a band for the
labels of your annotations. Dashed teal lines are the original events of the experiment, which
cannot be changed. Hatched grey stretches mark where the display filters may distort the signal (at
the start and end of the recording and around interruptions) and where the recording has no data.
Below the EEG, the overview bar shows the whole recording with your annotations and the part on
screen; click it to go anywhere.

Two rows of controls sit above the EEG. The first has Draw/edit, Delete, Undo, Redo, the time your
draft was last kept (for example "Draft saved 14:02"), Pause, Help and Save and Finish. Delete, Undo
and Redo are greyed out when they have nothing to act on: Delete until an annotation is selected,
Undo and Redo when there is no change to take back or make again. Draw/edit is on while you
annotate; pressed, it switches to view only, in which nothing can be drawn or changed by accident,
and the second row reads "View only" until you press it again. The second chooses what you mark next: the numbered type buttons, 0 Uncertain and the menu of all
types after "Draw as", then Whole montage or Selected channels with Channels… and Clear channel
selection, Overlay, and the scales: Time, the seconds on the screen, and Amplitude, the height of a
row of EEG, each with − and + beside it. Each type button shows its number and a short name (1
Blink, 2 HEM, 3 EMG and so on); when the window is too narrow for one line, the toolbar continues on
a second. On the right, the Annotations tab
lists everything you have marked, the Recording tab shows the time you have worked, how much of the
recording you have viewed, the viewing options, your comfort choices and details of the recording,
and the Help tab lists every key. The line at the bottom reminds you of the main keys and shows what
each action did and what to do next, on two lines when it needs them, so that an instruction is
always read to its end. On its right it says how much of the recording you have viewed and how many
annotations you have made, with the display filters below.

## Marking an artifact

First choose the type: press a number key from 1 to 9, click one of the numbered buttons, or choose
from the menu next to them. Press / to search all 29 types by part of their name or by their code
(for example "blink" or "103") and press Enter. Then choose whether the artifact affects the whole
montage or only some channels, with the switch in the second row or the key C. For selected
channels, click their names (or their traces); they turn magenta. To select several neighbouring
channels at once, drag down (or up) their names at the left of the EEG, as you would select files
on a computer: the channels from the first name to the last are selected, and the drag switches to
Selected channels by itself. Past the first or last row on the screen the channels scroll on, so a
drag can reach channels below the screen of a 256-channel montage. Hold Ctrl (⌘ on a Mac) while you
drag to add a range to the channels already selected, and Shift+click a name to select every
channel from the name you clicked last. Clear channel selection, beside the channels in the toolbar,
or the key X, deselects them all before you choose a new set. Channels… opens a list to choose from
instead.

The list is built for montages of any size. Type in its search field to find channels by name:
names or their beginnings, separated by commas, so that b1 finds B1 and B10 to B19, and a term
that begins no name finds the names that contain it (z finds Fz, Cz and Pz). Put a name in quotes
to find that channel only ("b1"), or use * for any characters (*z). With more than 16 channels, the
channels are laid out in columns, and buttons above them tick a whole group with one click, or
clear it when it is all ticked: the regions (frontal, central, temporal, parietal and occipital)
and sides of a montage named by the 10-20 or 10-10 system, the banks of numbered channels of other
montages (BioSemi's A1 to A32, B1 to B32 and so on, or EGI's E1 to E256 in blocks of 32), and the
types of channel when there are several. A montage named by numbers that comes with its own
electrodes.tsv (see below) has regions and sides as well, beside its banks: each electrode joins the
region and side of the nearest site of the 10-10 system. Show chosen only lists just the channels
ticked.

Map, at the top of the list, shows the channels as electrodes on a drawing of the head, seen from
above with the nose at the top, each a small circle with its name. Click an electrode to choose it
or take it back, or drag a box round several to choose them all (a box round electrodes that are
all chosen takes them back). Select all, Clear all and Invert act on every electrode, or on those your
search finds; the search and the group buttons work as in the list, and the two always show the same
choice. With the keyboard, the arrow keys move from an electrode to its neighbour in that direction
and Space or Enter chooses it. The electrodes go where the recording's own BIDS electrodes.tsv puts
them, when that file comes with the recording: when you open the recording together with it
(choose its folder, or the file with the recording's files), when it was kept with the recording's
copy on this device, or when it came with a recording opened from a link (see Opening a recording
from a link). Otherwise they go at the standard places of their 10-20, 10-10 or 10-05 names. A montage
whose names say nothing about where the electrodes are, such as BioSemi's A1 to H32 or EGI's E1 to
E256, is arranged on rings in the order of its channels, and the map says "Positions not known:
arranged in order". Channels that are not on the head (EOG, ECG and the like) are under it, as
Other channels. A bad channel is grey with a dashed edge and can still be chosen. When channels are
coloured by group, the map uses the same colours. The map opens with the whole head in view. On a
dense cap, such as 128 channels with 10-05 names or a 256-channel net, the electrodes are then too
close together for their names: each is a small dot, and its name shows when you point at it or move
to it with the arrow keys. The + button makes the map larger, step by step, until every electrode has
its name, and − makes it smaller again; a large map scrolls in its frame. In a short window the map
is made lower, so that the count of channels chosen under it stays in view without scrolling. ElectroTag remembers whether you last
used the list or the map. When Edit channels… opens on an annotation of the whole montage,
choosing a channel, in the list or on the map, switches it to "Only these channels" by itself.

Then drag across the EEG from the start of the artifact to its end. A drag that starts inside
another annotation draws a new one as well. With the keyboard, point at the start and press M, then
point at the end and press M again. You can scroll in between, which is the easy way to mark an
artifact longer than the screen; a note at the foot of the EEG ("Marking Eye-blink-artifact from
4.204 s") keeps the start in view while it waits for the end, and gives the time to the sample, as
it is saved. The keys [ and ] mark the start and the end separately, and Esc cancels a started mark.
You can also draw first and choose the type afterwards: click the new annotation and press a number,
or double-click it to choose any type.

When you can see an artifact but cannot tell what kind it is, mark it Uncertain: press 0, or click
0 Uncertain after the numbered types (with annotations selected, 0 changes them to it). It is saved
as Data-artifact, the HED-SCORE term for an artifact whose type is unknown, and it is treated as
needing review: the Annotations list says "needs review" under it, its label on the EEG reads
Uncertain, and the study's experts find it listed in their Saved work and adjudication queue so
that they can look at it again. When you know the family of an artifact but not its exact type,
choose the group term instead, such as Eye-artifact or Electrical-artifact, from the menu of all
types, where group terms are in italics. Uncertain always has the key 0; it is never among the
numbered types.

Without a mouse, M, [ and ] mark at the time cursor, the thin line a click on empty EEG places. Press
G and type a time to put the cursor there, and move it with the , and . keys (with Shift, ten times
as far). The EEG has a place in the Tab order, with a ring round it when it has the keyboard. When
the pointer rests on the EEG, the keys mark at the pointer, unless you moved the time cursor since.

In the overlay (below), every new annotation applies to the whole montage, since the channels share one axis
and a drag cannot say which of them it means; the toolbar says "all channels in the overlay", and
Selected channels waits until you press O for the normal view again.

## Changing annotations

Each row of the Annotations list shows the whole label on its first line and, on the line below,
the channels (or Whole montage), the rating and note sign when there are any, and the start and end
in seconds; hold the pointer over a row for the type's code and description. Click an annotation to
select it, or pick it in the Annotations list. While annotations are
selected the second row reads "Change to", and a number key, a type button or the menu changes their
type. Esc, a click on empty EEG or a new drag ends the selection, and the row reads "Draw as" again.
N and P select the next and previous annotation and bring it into view. With nothing selected, N
starts from the first annotation that reaches into the screen; after the last annotation N comes
round to the first, and P after the first to the last.

Drag an edge of an annotation to make it longer or shorter, and hold Shift while dragging its middle
to move it; the line at the bottom gives its new times. To grab an edge, point at its line: a drag
that starts just outside an annotation draws a new annotation instead, so that a second occurrence
just after the first is marked on its own. For a precise start or end, select the annotation and
press Alt (Option on a Mac) with ← or → to move its start 10 milliseconds earlier or later (at
least one sample), and Alt+Shift with ← or → to move its end; each press is one step that Undo
takes back, and the line at the bottom gives the new time to the thousandth of a second. To change the channels of an annotation,
select it and Shift+click a channel anywhere within its time, on the row of a channel it does not
have yet as well as on its own: that channel is added or taken out. With a channel-specific
annotation selected, a click on a channel's name does the same. Taking a channel out of a
whole-montage annotation leaves every other channel, and putting it back makes it whole montage
again. An annotation keeps at least one channel, so to remove its last channel, delete it or choose
Apply to whole montage. While an annotation is selected, clicking names changes that annotation and
leaves the channels for your next annotation as they were; Esc ends the selection. Right-click an
annotation for its menu: the common types, Other type for any of the 29, Edit times…, Edit
channels…, Confidence, Add a note…, Apply to whole montage (for a channel-specific annotation) and
Delete. Delete or Backspace removes the selected annotation, Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Y redoes (⌘
instead of Ctrl on a Mac). When you draw or change an annotation so that it overlaps another of the
same type on the same channels, the two are joined into one, and the line at the bottom says so
("Merged with 1 overlapping EMG-artifact annotation …"); Ctrl+Z takes the join back with the change
that caused it. Only a true overlap joins: annotations with a gap between them, however brief, and
annotations that only touch stay separate, so separate occurrences of one type keep their own
annotations. Your study can switch joining off, and then overlapping annotations stay as you drew
them. In the Annotations list you can also edit the start and end times exactly
(as seconds, 12.5, or as m:ss, 1:05.25, or h:mm:ss, the forms Go to time takes), change the type of
several annotations at once, and merge annotations of one type. The buttons for these appear once an
annotation is selected.

## Bad channels

A channel can be unusable for the whole of a recording, for example when its electrode lost contact
from the start. Mark it bad for the whole recording: right-click its name at the left of the EEG (on
an iPad, press and hold the name) and choose Mark as bad for the whole recording, or choose it in
Channels… and press Mark chosen as bad. Its row is hatched, its trace drawn in grey and its name
struck through; the Annotations tab counts the channels marked bad, and the list in Channels…
strikes them through. The same menu, or Mark chosen as good in Channels…, takes the mark off again,
and Ctrl+Z undoes it like any other change. Channels marked bad are kept in your draft and saved
with your annotations, in the BIDS way: the saved files list every channel with status good or bad
(see the file formats guide). A channel marked bad stays on the screen and can still be annotated,
which the study's analyses report apart.

Your study may already list some channels as bad in the recording's own channels.tsv. Those are
greyed and named "(bad)", as before, and stay as the study set them: they cannot be marked or
unmarked here, and your marks never change the study's list.

## Confidence and notes

You can rate how confident you are that a mark is right, on a scale from 1 to 5: 1 means not at all
confident, 2 slightly confident, 3 moderately confident, 4 very confident and 5 completely
confident. Press D and then a number from 1 to 5: the rating goes to the selected annotation, or to
the annotation you made last when nothing is selected. 0 clears the rating and Esc cancels. While
ElectroTag waits for the number, the line at the bottom says so ("Confidence for Eye-blink-artifact:
press 1–5, 0 to clear, Esc to cancel"), the annotation is outlined, a small bar with the numbers
appears beside it, under the band of labels, and the number keys rate rather than choose a type; the
wait ends with the number, Esc or another key, and never by itself. You can also right-click the annotation (press and hold it on an iPad), choose
Confidence and pick a number, with its meaning beside it, or Clear; or select the annotation in the
Annotations list and tap one of the numbers 1 to 5 below the list. Undo takes back a rating like any
other change.

A new annotation never starts with a rating, so that no rating is given without a thought. In most
studies rating is optional: rate an annotation when you want to, and leave the others as they are.
Some studies ask for a rating on every annotation: there, after each new one, it is outlined and the
bar of numbers appears beside it, and stays until you answer. Click or tap a number on the bar, or
press D and then the number. The number keys on their own keep choosing the type, as everywhere
else, so a rating is never given by a key meant for a type. You can carry on working without
answering, and Later or Esc puts the bar away; the annotation is then left unrated, and Save and
Finish lists those without a rating ("2 annotations have no confidence"), with Rate them, which
takes you to each in turn and waits for its number (here the number keys rate), Save anyway and
Keep annotating. An annotation without a rating looks exactly as annotations always have. The tag of
a rated annotation shows its rating, such as 3/5, annotations rated 1 or 2 are filled more lightly
on the EEG, and the Annotations list shows the rating with its meaning when you hold the pointer
over it.

Any annotation can also carry a short note, of up to 200 characters on one line, for example "could
be an eye movement rather than a blink". Choose Add a note… in the annotation's menu or below the
Annotations list, or write it in Edit times…, which shows the note beside the start and end. An
annotation with a note has a small note sign in the list and in its tag on the EEG; hold the
pointer over it to read the note, and choose Edit note… to change or remove it. Notes are saved
with your annotations for the study's researchers, who use them, with the confidence ratings, to
understand where annotators disagree. The record of how you use ElectroTag keeps only that a note
was added, changed or removed and how long it is, never what it says.

## Moving around the recording

The arrow keys ← and → scroll back and forward through time (with Shift, a whole screen), and ↑
and ↓ scroll through the channels. Home and End show less or more time, Page Up and Page Down show
fewer or more channels, and − and + make the traces smaller or larger. On a Mac keyboard without
Home, End and Page keys, use fn with the arrow keys.

The mouse wheel changes the amplitude: up makes the traces larger and down smaller, one notch as much
as one press of + or −, and each channel keeps its place, so the trace under the pointer stays where
it is. Shift with the wheel moves along time, as in many programs for viewing signals, and so does
moving two fingers sideways on a trackpad. Ctrl (⌘ on a Mac) with the wheel, or a pinch on a
trackpad, shows more or less time, keeping the moment under the pointer where it is. Alt (Option)
with the wheel scrolls through the channels. Until 30 October 2026 the wheel scrolled through time
and Shift+wheel through the channels; the change follows the annotators' requests.

The scales are also in the toolbar. Time shows the seconds on the screen: choose 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30
or 60 seconds, or press − or + beside it to step through the same choices as Home and End; the
middle of the screen stays where it is. After a pinch the time shown may be another, which the
choice then lists. Amplitude shows the height of one row of EEG, as the scale bar at the right of
the EEG gives it (for example 100 µV), with − and + beside it. Both follow every way of changing
them, the keys, the wheel and the touch buttons.

To go to a particular time, press G and type it: seconds (12.5), minutes and seconds (1:05) or
hours, minutes and seconds (1:02:05), then Enter. The time comes to the middle of the screen, with
the time cursor on it; a time past the end of the recording shows its end. If ElectroTag cannot read
what you typed, it says so under the field, and Esc closes the field. Show all, in the same field or
with Shift+End, puts the whole recording on the screen to find your way around it; press it again to
go back to the view you had. A view of the whole recording is an overview, so it does not count
towards the parts you have viewed, and a line at the top of the EEG says so for as long as it lasts.
On a long recording with many channels, reading the whole of it can take a while (about half a
minute for an hour of 256 channels): the EEG is drawn from the start as it is read, and a note over
it says how much has been read so far, for example "Reading the EEG… 35%". Going back to your view
does not wait for the rest. The overview keeps only what the screen can show, the lowest and highest
value of each short stretch, so even an hour of 256 channels takes little of the computer's or iPad's
memory; the value under the pointer is shown again once you go back to a view of the signal itself.

While the pointer is over a trace, the line at the bottom shows the channel, the time to the
thousandth of a second and the value of the signal there in µV, as drawn: filtered as you see it,
measured from the trace's centre line (its average over the screen), with positive values upwards.
V switches it off and on, and so does Value under the pointer in the Recording tab. It is on with a
mouse; on an iPad it starts off, and when switched on it follows your finger or Pencil while it
draws.

Overlay, in the toolbar, or the key O, draws all the channels on screen on one shared axis in thin,
light lines, in the channels' group colours when channels are coloured. An artifact that reaches
many channels at once, such as a blink or a movement, then stands out as a dark band, and one that
stays on a single channel stands apart from the rest. The time axis, your annotations, the events and
the overview bar stay as they are, and a scale bar on the right gives the size of half the axis. The
channel names on the left become a legend: point at one to see that channel drawn on top, and click
it, or press I, to see it alone; the arrow keys ↑ and ↓ then move to the previous and next channel,
and I or a click on the name again shows them all. Use Page Up and Page Down to overlay fewer or more
channels. ElectroTag remembers whether you last used the overlay, in this browser.

The Recording tab holds the viewing options: the 1 Hz high-pass filter and the notch filter for
mains interference (50 Hz in the UK and Europe, 60 Hz in North America). They change only what you
see; the file is never changed. Some studies fix these settings, and the tab then says so.

Colour channels by, in the same place, colours each channel's trace and name by its group, which
makes it easier to keep track of where a channel sits in a dense montage. By region, frontal
channels are blue, central green, temporal violet, parietal mauve and occipital blue again (the two
are at opposite ends of the head, and regions that touch never share a colour); by side, channels
on the left (odd numbers) are blue, those on the right (even numbers) green, and the midline (z)
stays black; by type, EOG is blue, ECG green, EMG violet and other channels mauve, and the EEG stays
black. A small legend under the choice says what each colour means and how many channels have it.
The colours only tell groups apart: a bad channel stays grey and is labelled "(bad)", the channels
you select stay pink, and no colour stands for a problem. Regions and sides follow the 10-20 and
10-10 names, or, for a montage named otherwise, such as BioSemi's A1 to H32 or EGI's E1 to E256, the
positions in the recording's own electrodes.tsv (each channel takes the region and side of the
nearest 10-10 site); without that file such a montage stays black by region and side.
ElectroTag remembers your choice in this browser; until you choose, the study's default applies
(none, unless the study team set one).

## Seeing the whole recording

ElectroTag keeps track of the parts of the recording that have been on the screen, so that none
is missed in a long recording. A part counts as viewed once it has stayed on the screen for a moment
while you are working. On the overview bar under the EEG, the parts not yet viewed are white with a
thin grey line above them, and the parts viewed keep the bar's usual warm grey. The Recording tab
and the line at the bottom say how much you have viewed, for example "Viewed 64%"; stretches where
the recording has no data count as viewed, since there is nothing to see there.

Next part not yet viewed, in the Recording tab, or the key U, takes you to the next part you have
not seen after the screen, coming round from the start when there are none further on, with a
second of what you saw before it. On an iPad it is the last of the buttons under the EEG. What you
have viewed is kept with your draft, so it is still there when you continue later.

## A part of a recording

The study team may give you only part of a recording, so that several people each annotate a
different stretch of a long one. The top of the annotation view then names your part, for example
Your part: 0:20 to 0:40, and the rest of the recording is hatched on the EEG and on the overview
bar. The view stays within your part, with a second either side so that you can judge an artifact
at its edge. Mark only inside your part: a mark that runs past its edge stops at the edge, and one
outside it is not made, with a message that says so. How much you have viewed, and Next part not yet
viewed, count your part alone. Everything else works as for a whole recording, and Save and Finish
saves your part.

## Pausing and breaks

Annotating a recording takes a long time, and the time you work on each recording is part of the
study's results. Press Pause, at the top, or the key B when you step away: the clock that measures
your time stops, your draft is kept, and a calm panel covers the EEG with the time worked so far and
a large Resume button. Press Resume, or B again, to carry on. While you are paused nothing can be
changed by accident: Draw/edit, Delete, Undo and Redo at the top wait for Resume too, and the Pause
button reads Resume.

ElectroTag also pauses by itself in two cases. After a few minutes without any input (three
unless your study chose otherwise), it pauses and says so; the minutes before that pause still count,
since you may have been reading the EEG, and the panel says how much time since then is left out. The pause starts when those minutes run out, so a computer
that went to sleep with the recording on screen comes back paused from then, without the night. When the page is out of sight for more than ten seconds,
because you switched to another tab or app or the iPad locked, it pauses from the moment the page
was hidden. When you come back, the panel says how long you were away and that the time was not
counted.

After a long stretch of work without a break (50 minutes unless your study chose otherwise), a small
note at the foot of the side panel suggests a break, where it covers none of the EEG. Take a break pauses straight away; Remind me in 15
minutes puts the note away for another quarter of an hour of work. The note never stops you working,
and any pause of five minutes or more counts as a break. The time since your last break carries over
from one recording to the next.

Pausing works the same way in guided training, where the coach waits behind the panel and the time
paused is left out of the time a step took. The short-segment quiz has no Pause, since it measures
how long you take to answer each segment.

## Comfort for long sessions

The Recording tab has a Comfort panel with choices of your own, kept in the browser you use and
applied to every recording you open there. Traces can be thin, normal or thick; thick traces are
easier to follow on a bright screen or from a little further away. Channel names can be normal or
large, and the text of the side panel normal or larger. Names are never drawn smaller than they can
be read: when the rows are too short for a name each (many channels on the screen, or a small
window), every second, third or, with 256 channels, every fourth or fifth channel is named, the
channels you have selected are named too, and pointing at a row names its channel. When you have
selected so many channels that their names would cover each other, as many are named as fit, spread
over them. Page Up shows fewer channels, each with its name. With only a few channels, such as one
or two, the rows keep the height they have with a handful (at most 120 pixels each) and sit in the
middle of the EEG, rather than stretching one trace over the whole height. These choices change only how the EEG and
the panel look, never what is saved. ElectroTag also follows your computer's or iPad's setting
to reduce motion, and announces its messages to screen readers.

## On an iPad

Everything described here works on an iPad, with your fingers or an Apple Pencil, in Safari or
another browser. One finger or the Pencil works as the mouse does: drag across the EEG to mark an
artifact, tap an annotation to select it, drag its edge to make it longer or shorter, and tap a
channel's name or its trace to choose that channel. Two fingers move the EEG: sideways through
time and up and down through the channels; pinch sideways to show more or less time. Press and
hold an annotation for its menu, the one a right-click opens on a computer, which also offers Edit
times… to move it, Confidence and Add a note…; double-tap it to choose its type from the list
of all types. Press and hold a channel's name for its menu, to mark the channel bad for the whole
recording or to add it to the channel selection, and drag a finger down the names to select a range
of channels. The numbers 1 to 5 below the Annotations list rate the selected annotation with one
tap, and in a study that asks for a rating on every annotation a bar of numbers appears beside each
new one. A second finger
that touches the screen while you are marking cancels the mark, so a move never leaves an
annotation behind by accident.

Under the EEG a row of buttons does what the keys do on a computer: back and forward a screen or a
little, more or less time, earlier and later channels, more or fewer of them, and larger or smaller
traces, and one shows the next part not yet viewed. The View buttons switch the overlay on and off,
show one channel alone (with buttons for the previous and next channel), open Go to time and Show
all. Pause is at the top, as on a
computer. The numbered type buttons, 0 Uncertain, Whole montage and Selected channels, the Time and
Amplitude scales, Delete, Undo and Redo are in the toolbars as on a computer, and a keyboard attached to the iPad works with every key described
here. In Channels…, Map works by touch: tap an electrode to choose it, drag with the Pencil to
choose several in a box, and drag with a finger to move a map larger than its frame; on an iPad the
electrodes are large enough for a finger, so a dense montage opens as a larger map, centred on the
head. In the Recording tab every choice is a row large enough for a finger, and the 50 and 60 Hz
choices are set in under the notch filter they belong to. Held upright, the iPad shows the EEG across its width with the side panel (and the coach in
training) below it, and the Annotations tab puts its buttons beside the list so that several rows
stay in view; on its side, the panel is beside the EEG as on a computer.

To open a recording, choose Choose a file…, which opens the iPad's file picker (the Files app, with
iCloud Drive, the iPad itself and any drive plugged in). Tap Select in the picker to choose several
files, such as the three files of a BrainVision recording; the iPad cannot choose a whole folder.
Every file can be chosen, and ElectroTag checks that it is a recording of the study. Files you
download, such as your results in the demonstration, go to the Downloads folder in Files.

## Saving

Press Save and Finish when you have looked through the whole recording. If some parts of at least a
second have not been on the screen, ElectroTag first lists them with their times; Go to the first
part (Go to that part, when there is one) takes you there, Save anyway goes on, for example when
you reviewed them another way, and Keep annotating closes the list. Your study may turn this check off. ElectroTag then shows how many annotations of each type you
are saving and asks you to confirm; once saved, the recording is complete. You can still revise it
later from your history when your study allows revisions (see "History and revisions"); each
revision is saved as a new version. If you marked nothing, it asks whether to save the recording as
reviewed with no artifacts found. After saving, it offers the next recording.

Saving can take a few seconds when the study is slow to answer. Meanwhile the button reads Saving…,
pressing it again does nothing, and Close, Leave without changes and Pause wait for the answer, with
a line at the bottom that says so; no draft is sent while the save is under way. If the answer never
arrives (the connection dropped, or the study failed once), the recording stays open and you can try
again. For a revision, the study may have saved it all the same: when you submit again, ElectroTag
then says that the revision was most likely saved the first time and takes you to your history,
where the latest version shows, rather than saving it twice.

Your work is saved as a draft while you annotate, in the browser and with the study, so closing the
page by accident loses nothing; the time at the top of the view says when the draft was last kept.
When the study cannot be reached, for example because the connection dropped, the top reads "Draft
kept in this browser only" on a pale amber ground, and the line at the bottom says so once. Your
work is safe in this browser meanwhile, and the draft goes to the study as soon as the connection
returns; the line then says that the study has your latest draft. Until then, the draft cannot
follow you to another browser. Save and Finish is never sent again by itself: press it again once
the connection is back. The saved time worked on the recording leaves out every pause.

‹ Close asks whether to close and finish later, which keeps your draft with your annotations and
time, or to discard them, which asks a second time. When you open a recording that has a draft
from the home page's list or with Open a recording from your computer, ElectroTag asks whether to
restore it. Esc or a click beside the question restores it, as Restore and continue does, and Start
afresh asks a second time before it deletes the draft ("Delete your unfinished work?", with Keep
them as the safe answer). Work deleted with Start afresh, Discard and close or Leave without changes
is kept in the browser tab until you close it: when you open the recording again in that tab,
a note in the Annotations tab offers Bring it back, which brings back its annotations, its time
worked and the parts it had viewed, as one change that Undo takes back. A revision asks the same
way ("Continue your revision?"), with Start again from version 1 behind a second question.

## On a phone

A phone has no room for the EEG traces, so annotating needs a tablet or a computer. On a phone the
sign-in page says so, and administrators can still sign in there to check the study's progress in
the admin area, which works on a phone. If you sign in as an annotator on a phone, the page offers
Email me this link, which starts an email to yourself with the address of the page (and of your
study) to open later on a tablet or computer, and Sign out, and it names the account you are signed
in with. Your work waits for you on the larger screen. An invitation link opened on a phone says this
before you choose a password, while the link is still unused.

On a computer the same notice appears when the browser window is very small, which is often because
the page has been made larger with the browser's zoom (at 200% on a laptop, for example). The
notice then says that Ctrl and minus (⌘ and minus on a Mac) makes room again, and offers Continue in
this window, which brings the views back for as long as the tab is open. In a short window the
annotation view keeps the EEG and the side panel at a usable height and scrolls down to the rest,
rather than drawing one part over another; at 150% on a laptop, the side panel stays beside the
EEG.

## The training programme

Trainees see their training path: five stages, each with its progress and result, and above them one
button to continue with the next step. Until the training is complete, signing in opens the training
path. The stages are Find your way around (a tour of ElectroTag), Meet the artifacts (examples of
each type), Learn with the experts, On your own and the Competency assessment. Each stage opens when
the one before it is finished or skipped. A stage your study lets you skip has Skip this stage: it
asks first, then marks the stage Skipped, and Do it after all brings it back whenever you like. The
study notes every skip, with its time, and the competency report lists the stages skipped. The
assessment can never be skipped. Once the tour or Meet the artifacts is done, Look again on its card
opens it once more, for example to look at the examples again before the assessment. When the path takes you to a recording, the page for opening it
names the stage the recording is for; your study team sends you the training recordings.

The tour, Find your way around, opens a practice recording that ElectroTag makes in the browser, so no
file is needed and nothing you mark is kept. A panel on the right takes you through six steps, each
naming the keys and buttons to use: moving through the recording with the arrow keys, marking an
outlined blink (its number key, then a drag across it), marking muscle activity on its channels
only (Selected channels and the channel names at the left of the EEG), taking a change back with
Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) or Undo and bringing it back with Ctrl+Y or Redo, saying how sure you are
(select a mark, press D and a number from 1 to 5) and Save and Finish. On an iPad every step is
written for touch instead: the arrow buttons beside Time under the EEG, taps on Undo and Redo, and
the Confidence numbers under the list of annotations (or pressing and holding the mark). The
artifacts to mark come after the first five seconds of the recording, where the hatching shows the
filters settling. Each step is ticked when you have done it, and Next step (or Enter) goes on;
Skip this step goes on without it. Save and Finish at the last step ends the tour and takes you back
to your training path. Close leaves the tour after asking; you can take it again from the path.

Meet the artifacts shows short examples of each type of artifact from your training set: the
experts' marks on the recordings you learn with, and any examples your study team added. Open your
copies of the recordings it names, as for the quiz (the demonstration offers its own copies). Each
example shows a few seconds of EEG with the artifact outlined on the channels it is on, its type, the
type's description and the experts' note; the time axis gives the recording's own times, as the text
does. Previous and Next (or the left and right arrow keys) step through them, and the list under
them, by family and type, jumps to any; the ones you have looked at are marked. On a short screen
the panel scrolls as a whole, and on an iPad held upright the example and the list sit under the
EEG. Take the recognition check opens the set's quiz with the recordings you have open, so nothing
needs opening again, and Back to the examples returns to the gallery as you left it, with your
result ("Recognition check: 3 of 4 right"). I have looked at them: finish this stage completes the
stage (some studies ask for the recognition check to be finished first, and say so). The examples
and the check never come from practice or assessment recordings, whose experts' marks are the
answers you are scored against.

In the third stage, Learn with the experts, a coach on the right
takes you through the experts' annotations of a recording one at a time. In the first recording the
experts' annotation is outlined with a dashed line for you to copy; in the next you find each
artifact on the screen yourself. For each one the coach says what to do with the controls in front
of you: press the type's number (or click the numbered button next to Draw as), make sure Whole
montage or Selected channels is chosen and, for selected channels, click the names of the channels
at the left of the EEG, then drag across the artifact. It checks your work after every change and
says what to put right and how: the type (select your annotation and press the right number), the
start or the end (drag that edge, and which way), or the channels (right-click your annotation for
Edit channels… or Apply to whole montage, or hold Shift and click it on a channel to take that
channel off). On an iPad the coach speaks of taps, press and hold and the buttons on screen instead.
When your annotation matches, press Continue (or Enter). Show me (or H) places the experts'
annotation for you. When the coach holds more than fits, More below under it scrolls on. Under the
instructions, a note says what the artifact looks like and why the experts marked it on those
channels. Each artifact you match yourself earns up to three stars, fewer after several tries, and
one when you used Show me. Save and Finish becomes available once every artifact matches. The time
each step took counts only while the recording is open: if you close it and come back later, the
step's time carries on from your last action on it.

A study can make a guided recording an exercise in correcting the marks instead. The recording then
opens with a copy of the experts' marks in which some are deliberately wrong: a mark left out, a mark
of a type it is easily mistaken for, a start or an end moved, or a mark where there is no artifact.
The panel on the right says how many mistakes there are, never which. Correct them as you would your
own marks (change a type, drag an edge, delete a mark, mark what is missing), then press Save and
Finish. ElectroTag then shows your saved marks on the EEG, read only, beside a list of each planted
mistake ("Left out: fixed", "Wrong type: not fixed") and any new mistake you made (a right mark you
changed, or a mark where the experts marked nothing); press an item, or N and P, to go to it on the
EEG, and Back to my training path when you are done. The recording counts as done whatever the
result, as this stage is for learning, and the stage's card keeps the result ("Correct the marks:
you fixed 2 of 4 planted mistakes.").

In the fourth stage, On your own, you annotate a practice recording without help. After you save it,
ElectroTag shows your result in plain words: whether you reached the pass mark, your score, and what
the score is made of, for example "You found 7 of the experts' 8 artifacts and made 3 marks they did
not make." The score is highest when you find every artifact the experts marked, with the right
type, and make no marks they did not make; each missed artifact, extra mark or wrong type lowers it.
The pass mark, set by the study (80% unless it says otherwise), needs both a score of at least that
much and the right type for at least that share of the artifacts you found. Under the result, your
annotations are shown next to the experts' on the EEG, labelled You and Experts, with every
disagreement marked: Missed for an artifact you did not mark, Extra for a mark the experts did not
make, and "Should be …" for one you gave another type. The list "What to look at" takes you to each
of them, and N and P step through it. The figures behind the score (such as the share of the
experts' artifacts you found with their type) are under "The figures behind the score". If you do
not pass, your training path offers a practice recording again: when there are several, the one you
practised longest ago, so that you do not repeat answers you have just seen; with only one, the same
recording.

The last stage, the Competency assessment, unlocks when you have passed the practice (or skipped
the stages before it). You annotate
its recordings as in the second stage but without feedback, and nothing tells you how you did until
every recording of the assessment is saved. Then the training path shows the result: Criteria met,
Criteria not met yet (and your study administrator goes through the results with you before you try
again), or Result to come, when the study team has to judge it. When you pass, the line above the
stages says "You passed: your study recordings are now open." with Open next recording beside it.
A stage that your programme does not use is marked Not included.

Most studies keep their own recordings locked until you pass the assessment. Until then your
recordings are listed on your home page under Locked, with "Opens once you pass the competency
assessment" under the file name, and a line with a lock on your training path and your home page
says why; they open by themselves once you pass. If your assessment did not meet the criteria, your
training path, your home page and those recordings say "Assessment not met: waiting for your study
team". Your study team goes through the results with you, then either reopens your training or gives
you a new assessment. After a reopening your path shows the stages to do again, which cannot be
skipped this time; what you save from then on counts, what you saved before is kept, and the
assessment's card lists the earlier round with its result. With a new assessment, the stages you
did or skipped before are shown as they were ("Skipped in Pilot training set"), and the new
competency report lists those skips too. Experts and administrators never wait.

Once every stage is done, ElectroTag opens on your home page when you sign in, with Open next
recording for the study's own work and a line that takes you back to your training path. The
recordings of your training are counted on the training path, apart from the study's.

### Watching an expert at work

When the study shows experts' sessions to trainees and an expert has a session to show, Watch an
expert at work appears on the Learn with the experts stage of your training path once that stage is
open. It lists the recordings you may watch: training recordings for learning with the experts, on
which experts who agreed to their sessions being used for teaching have worked. You never see an
expert at work on a recording of the study itself, which you may be given to annotate on your own.
Watch, then open the recording's file from your computer as
you would to annotate it (your study team gives you the files for this), and the expert's session
plays back on the EEG: the view moves as theirs did, a small blue ring shows their pointer, their
annotations appear as they made them, and a line under the EEG says what they did ("Pressed M: Mark
start, then end", "Created Eye-blink-artifact by dragging on the EEG"). Space plays and pauses, - and
= change the speed, the , and . keys step from one event to the next, < and > (or N and P) from one
change of the annotations to the next, and Home goes back to the start; Skip pauses (I) shortens the moments
when nothing happened, and when you move through the EEG yourself, Follow again (F) goes back to
the expert's view; ? lists these keys. Notes and anything the expert typed are never recorded, so a
note shows only its length. Back returns to your home page.

### How everyone marked a training recording

Some studies also let trainees see how everyone who worked on a training recording for learning
with the experts marked it. Then How everyone marked it appears on the Learn with the experts stage
once someone has saved one of those recordings. It lists them, with how many people saved each and
whether you are among them; See opens the recording's file from your computer, as for watching an
expert. A thin band under each channel is shaded by the share of people who marked each moment on
that channel, the darker the more of them, and the Crowd lane above the EEG does the same for marks
on the whole montage. Rest the pointer on a band to read the share and the types people chose there.
Nobody is named, and you only ever see this on those training recordings, never on a recording of
the study itself, so that your own study work stays your own judgement. N and P go to the next and
previous moment where people disagree, the filters show one type at a time, and Download the summary
(TSV) saves the view as a table.

## The short-segment quiz

Some studies also offer a quiz, listed on your first page (and on the training path for trainees)
with its number of segments and when you learn your results: whether each answer is right,
straight away, or your result at the end. A quiz shows short pieces of EEG, each with a stretch
shaded in blue, and the question "Which artifact type is the main one in the shaded segment?". It
tests whether you recognise the types; marking where an artifact starts and ends is assessed
separately, in whole recordings.

The segments come from recordings of the study, so the quiz first asks you to open them from your
computer, as for annotation: choose the files, drop them onto the page, or choose a folder that
holds them (one folder may hold all of them). Each file is checked against the study's copy by its
fingerprint, and a file whose contents differ is refused with an explanation. The page lists the
recordings the quiz needs and which one it needs now.

Each segment is shown with a few seconds of EEG around it, read only, with the study's filters. The
answers are listed by family, as in the annotation view, with group terms such as Eye-artifact
marked "group: exact type unclear"; some quizzes also offer "No artifact / none of these". Choose
one and press Submit answer (or Enter); the description of the type you chose appears under the
list, and How the quiz works, at the top of the page for opening the recordings, sums this up.
Depending on the quiz, you see straight away whether it was right, with a short explanation, and
press Next segment; or you go on to the next segment and see your result at the end. Every answer is
kept with the study as soon as you send it, together with the time you took to give it, and cannot
be changed afterwards: if you answer a segment on two pages or computers, the first answer is kept
and the page says so. ‹ Stop the quiz offers two choices: Stop for now keeps your answers so that
you can carry on another time from where you stopped, and End the quiz now finishes it with the
score of the segments you answered. At the end ElectroTag shows how many segments you answered
correctly, overall and for each artifact type (after End the quiz now, under the heading Quiz ended
early). The quiz's entry on your first page says how far a stopped quiz went, with Continue the
quiz, and a finished quiz has See my result, which shows the same result again.

## The demonstration

Without a study, ElectroTag runs as a demonstration in the browser. Sign in as A01 to annotate or
as T01 to follow the training programme; the password is demo. Three example recordings with
simulated artifacts are built in: use one straight away, or download them as EDF files and open
them from your computer to try that way too. A quiz of seven segments from the first two examples,
with feedback after each answer, is on the first page for both accounts, under the name of the
Demonstration training set; Use the
example recordings opens its recordings without any files. Your results stay in your browser;
Download my results saves them as a file, and Reset the demonstration forgets them. Wherever the
demonstration does what a study's server would do (checking guided steps, scoring practice, storing
results), the page says so.

## If something goes wrong

If the page says the study does not answer, check your internet connection and try again; if it
keeps happening, tell your study team. Once your browser has reached a study at its own address, a
later visit when the study does not answer says so in this way, rather than opening the
demonstration. If a file is not recognised although it should be, make sure
you chose the study's copy of the recording and, for BrainVision, all three of its files. If a link
does not open, download the file with the button ElectroTag offers and choose it, or ask your
study team for a link that anyone with it can open. If your
sign-in has ended, sign in again: your unsaved work is still in the browser.
